# Year 7 Term 4 outcome coverage

Every row links to a working lesson mode through `/#/simulators/CODE`. The default `/` and `/#/` menu presents these nineteen entries in the supplied syllabus sections and numeric order. It shows the exact wording and direct activity links without placing a long syllabus block over the experiment. Entries describe the activity students can perform; each model’s assumptions and reference limits are linked below.

| Outcome | Exact supplied wording | Direct launch | Working simulation or investigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1.1 | Identify the 3 main states of matter and how they are represented in the movement of water on earth | `/#/simulators/4.1.1` | Water cycle landscape tracks liquid, ice, cloud droplets and invisible water vapour through heating, cooling and weather-driven transfers. The States comparison remains one click away. |
| 4.1.2 | Conduct an investigation to measure and graph the temperature of water to identify the changes of state as heated over time | `/#/simulators/4.1.2` | Select heating equipment and thermometer, set heater power, run model time, take readings, inspect the temperature-time graph and record/export data. |
| 4.1.3 | Represent changes in the state of matter in terms of particle arrangement and movement | `/#/simulators/4.1.3` | Change Station drives all six changes of state through energy or surface conditions while preserving the H₂O sample and phase ledger. |
| 4.1.4 | Compare the properties of matter in different states, including the relative strength of attractive forces between solid, liquid and gas particles, to explain differences in the behaviours of the 3 states of matter | `/#/simulators/4.1.4` | Compare the same number of water molecules in solid, liquid and gas models; observe arrangement, movement, shape response and qualitative relative-attraction cues. |
| 4.2.1 | Investigate the other physical properties of water, such as density, buoyancy and surface tension | `/#/simulators/4.2.1` | Separate density, displacement-based buoyancy and qualitative surface-tension interactions into selectable investigations. |
| 4.2.2 | Conduct a practical investigation and select appropriate equipment to measure the density of water and other substances, and record the results in a table to compare the calculated density with SI data | `/#/simulators/4.2.2` | Tare a balance, choose liquid/volume tools, read a measured volume and mass, calculate density and compare recorded trials with reference conditions. |
| 4.2.3 | Determine the volume and mass of regular-shaped and irregular-shaped objects to calculate their density using the formula image | `/#/simulators/4.2.3` | Measure dimensions for a regular object or displacement for an irregular object, weigh, calculate density = mass ÷ volume and record the SI conversion/reference. |
| 4.3.1 | Investigate what substances dissolve in water and discuss findings using key terms, including soluble, insoluble, solubility, solute, solvent and solution | `/#/simulators/4.3.1` | Add soluble or insoluble samples to water, stir and inspect the changing mass ledger and particle view with the key terms in context. |
| 4.3.2 | Conduct and document a practical investigation to measure solubility of different solutes in water, and present data using tables and relevant graphs | `/#/simulators/4.3.2` | Measure fixed-volume trials, distinguish lower bounds from equilibrium-with-excess measurements, record rows and graph the evidence. |
| 4.3.3 | Qualitatively investigate the effect of temperature on solubility | `/#/simulators/4.3.3` | Vary temperature for the selected solute and compare its illustrative, substance-dependent capacity. |
| 4.3.4 | Describe how solutions can be modelled using particle theory | `/#/simulators/4.3.4` | Inspect dispersed sucrose molecules or dissociated ions among water molecules, with particle visibility and dissolution state linked to the mass ledger. |
| 4.3.5 | Compare the properties of dilute, concentrated, saturated and supersaturated solutions | `/#/simulators/4.3.5` | Compare concentration against a matched sample and saturation capacity; cool a seed-free supersaturated model, then add a nucleation seed. |
| 4.4.1 | Distinguish between atoms, mixtures and compounds and explain their properties using particle theory | `/#/simulators/4.4.1` | Classify O₂ molecules, Ar atoms, H₂O molecules and ionic/mixed samples while keeping particle entities distinct from composition classes. |
| 4.4.2 | Classify matter as pure substances, including elements and compounds, and impure substances, including mixtures based on their particle composition | `/#/simulators/4.4.2` | Compare one-substance element/compound samples with multi-substance mixtures and receive evidence-based feedback on the submitted classification. |
| 4.4.3 | Explain how the physical properties of substances are used to separate mixtures | `/#/simulators/4.4.3` | Select size, magnetism, density/immiscibility, insolubility or volatility; a property-matched apparatus transfers only eligible materials. |
| 4.4.4 | Conduct a series of practical investigations to explore common techniques to separate mixtures | `/#/simulators/4.4.4` | Run sieve, magnet, settling/decanting, filter, evaporation and distillation trials; record the method, property, recovered mass and remaining mass. |
| 4.4.5 | Investigate techniques used by Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander Peoples to separate mixtures | `/#/simulators/4.4.5` | Read source-attributed yandying context, adjust illustrative seed/chaff size and density, tilt and shake an unadorned dish, and compare with sieving. |
| 4.4.6 | Investigate an industrial separation technique | `/#/simulators/4.4.6` | Operate a simplified screening, coagulant/flocculation and filter-media sequence; compare particle capture and preserved pollutant mass. |
| 4.5.1 | Model how a body of water can become polluted, and plan and conduct a practical investigation that attempts to remove the pollutants | `/#/simulators/4.5.1` | Add floating, suspended and dissolved pollutants, set a hypothesis and treatment, run matched trials, and compare pollutant/waste/recovered-water ledgers and graph. |

The first row’s water-cycle outcome is represented by its own landscape and conserved reservoir ledger; it is no longer a partial States-only entry. The direct States route is 4.1.4. The full Matter group also keeps six direct transition routes (`/#/changes/melting`, `freezing`, `vaporisation`, `condensation`, `sublimation`, `deposition`) and optional atomic examples at `/#/advanced/plasma` and `/#/advanced/bec`.
